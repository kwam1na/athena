What this delivers

This candidate brings the already deployed Wigclub migration fixes and legacy sweep retirement into main. It increases close-evidence and rollup dispatch to the existing four-claim maximum, verifies supported historical accepted formats without rewriting them, and removes the obsolete store sweep.

Why migration stalled

One claim per dispatch slowed two durable queues. After those drained, accepted-history proof rejected a sealed correction and historical report shapes. More throughput could not resolve those semantic failures.

Verify the original evidence

An accepted report is an immutable historical receipt in its original format. Replay must establish the same financial truth and original fingerprint. It must not silently replace that receipt with a modern rendering.

Before and after

  1. Before: close-evidence and rollup each claimed one job. After: each can claim four independent fenced jobs.
  2. Before: parity assumed newer metrics and leader widths. After: supported historical forms are checked against original evidence.
  3. Before: the cron scheduled fourteen lanes including a legacy dispatcher. After: thirteen lanes remain, including maintenance.

Behavior boundaries

The supported correction is the existing sealed Wigclub August 3–9 repair, not arbitrary historical certification. Missing historical transaction metrics stay absent. Supported three-leader output still requires its original fingerprint. Unknown widths, foreign rows, altered correction payloads and inconsistent financial evidence fail.

Worker budgets, leases, source commands, accepted baselines, historical schemas, compatibility readers and shared maintenance remain. Summary-range workers retain their legacy-named evidence lane. Four claims do not establish fourfold throughput or measured billing savings.
