Executive summary

This candidate replaces three copied operating-date pickers with one shared picker and navigation control, then gives the Reports Items workspace matching compact day, week, and month arrows.

The shared controls make historical review quicker without changing the owner of reporting periods, operating-day lifecycle, queries, permissions, or backend data. Forward navigation stops at the current operating date.

Problem

Daily Operations, Daily Opening, and Daily Close each carried near-identical calendar picker code. Reports Items required selecting a date from its calendar even for a simple adjacent period. The duplicated controls could drift in their future-date guard, accessibility labels, and historical calendar focus.

Mental model

The shared controls are a steering wheel, not the map. `PeriodNavigation` renders arrows only; the workspace supplies labels, availability, arithmetic, and the callback that changes its own state. `OperatingDatePicker` supplies calendar presentation plus adjacent-day steering, while each operations view keeps ownership of its date search state and bounded query.

Before and after

Before, three operations views each rendered a calendar-only picker, and Reports Items had no adjacent-period affordance. After, operations share one picker with accessible previous/next day controls, while Reports computes adjacent day, week, or month values through its reporting-period helper.

Month movement preserves the selected day where possible and clamps to the final day in a short month. Day and week movement use operating-date helpers. Reports permits moving into a current partial week or month when that period starts on or before today, then clamps the selected anchor date to today; a wholly future period remains unavailable.

Changed and intentionally unchanged

Changed: reusable frontend presentation controls, operations reuse, reporting-period arithmetic, and regression tests for date boundaries, keyboard activation, and disabled states.

Unchanged: Convex APIs and mutations, reporting read-model scope, route/search parameters, opening and close readiness rules, authorization, storefront behavior, and production backend deployment requirements. The underlying calendar component is unchanged, while its initial month is now standardized around the selected historical date. The relevant later deployment is the Athena admin static app only.

Failure boundaries

The UI guard prevents future selection; it does not claim a historical operating day has data or alter server-side lifecycle rules. The extraction cannot change Daily Close approvals or history because the picker never owns those commands. A caller remains responsible for what a newly selected date loads and whether that date is actionable.

The calendar and next-day arrow share the same inclusive upper date limit, preventing one route into a future day while the other is blocked.

Next-time guidance

When another workspace needs adjacent operating dates, reuse the presentation controls but keep business-period arithmetic and query ownership in that workspace. Add tests for month-end, leap-year, and the current operating-date guard before treating a new navigation path as complete.

For a substantial Athena frontend candidate, create the solution note, reader report, and exact-candidate review evidence before expecting the authoritative merge gate to proceed.
