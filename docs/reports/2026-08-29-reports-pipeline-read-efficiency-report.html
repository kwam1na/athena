What this candidate delivers

The reports pipeline materializes compact frozen evidence once per generation, reuses validated reads within an execution, records exact work, and updates period totals from changed day contributions. Independent workers contain failures; publication gates prevent incomplete financial numbers from appearing complete.

Repeated local fixtures show 96.4% less returned payload for weekly refreshes and 62.7% less for period changes. Query calls increase, and weekly document reads increase. These are bandwidth proxies—not production billing, latency or savings claims.

Why the hotfix was not the finish line

PR #811 removed overview’s repeated full-close hydration. Weekly work still revisited large frozen close snapshots, and period rollups reread many unchanged SKU-day rows. Limiting the number of rows or splitting one function into several cannot, by itself, prove lower total read cost.

The design had to reduce recurring payload without losing quiet historical work, corrupting accepted reports, or exposing half-applied rollups.

Keep the receipt; update the contribution

Think of the canonical day fold as a signed-off receipt. It atomically saves the day, its SKU contributions, immutable worker inputs and the work still owed. A downstream worker changes a period by removing that day’s previous contribution and adding its new one. The period is readable only when every owed contribution is complete.

Source command → facts / compact invalidation
Day fold → canonical rows + immutable input + exact obligations → acknowledge dirty mark
Bounded workers → checkpoints → complete period → publish revision

Facts, stock and cash still commit through their existing operational transactions. This work moves derived reporting—not business correctness—behind background workers.

What changed—and what did not

Period readers now return ready, pending, blocked or restart states. Pending/blocked results contain no partial financial rows. Recovery keeps both new requests and issued-cursor requests pending; after the replacement epoch is activated and its period is ready, old epoch/publication cursors request a restart. Overview and inventory freshness remain explicit.

Accepted financial baselines, original observation cutoffs, notification dedupe, source authorization, inventoryLedger stock ownership and the pure day fold remain authoritative. A folded date refreshes every overlapping accepted frame after a schedule-anchor change, not just the newest. Unknown profit never becomes a fabricated zero. Existing fact/SKU cardinality support is preserved. Movement and mix requests retain their separate lifecycle.

The candidate also corrects one admission-checker false positive for literal database-reader chains. Unknown factories and hidden router registrations still fail closed. Two enlarged import cycles were removed without expanding the dependency baseline.

How incomplete or old state behaves

A failed data transaction rolls back; a separate transaction records retry evidence. A stale generation or lease cannot acknowledge newer work. Expired summary cleanup leaves the live queue before all children drain, and poisoned close cleanup retains foreign data while allowing later healthy headers to progress.

Migration builds a fresh epoch from zero and proves source-close, accepted-cutoff and rollup parity. Source/accepted watermarks and the control fence bind every proof page to activation. Unsupported historical corrections or lost observation-time evidence block activation; the code never repairs them by rewriting a frozen accepted report.

Reseed and deletion fence old workers. Purged epochs stop receiving fold obligations. Monotonic control activation history survives repeated resets; each migration separately chooses whether proved reactivation is automatic. Never-activated stores still require explicit first activation. Organization removal keeps retained stores paused. Rollback means a fresh, proved rebuild with pending readers—not switching stale legacy totals back on. An arbitrary older binary is not automatically compatible.

Check your understanding

Pass with nine of eleven answers. The questions test boundaries, not filenames.

  1. What establishes the optimization’s value?

    1. A lower byte count for sweep alone
    2. Lower whole-pipeline recurring bytes with all new overhead included
    3. A larger number of smaller workers

    Moving reads between functions is not a saving; dispatch, recovery and source-projection costs must be included.

  2. Why is compact close evidence stored outside reportDay?

    1. To let source commands skip facts
    2. To change accepted close totals
    3. To avoid multiplying frozen evidence across routine day/overview reads

    Frequently scanned day rows stay compact while specialized readers load the evidence they need.

  3. When may a day worker acknowledge its dirty mark?

    1. After canonical output, immutable input and exact handoffs commit together
    2. Before dispatching the fold
    3. As soon as one downstream worker starts

    The handoff belongs to the same transaction as canonical output so a retry cannot lose owed work.

  4. What should period readers show during a partial delta apply?

    1. The updated rows alongside stale totals
    2. A pending state without partial financial rows
    3. Zero totals until the worker finishes

    Neither a partial result nor a manufactured zero represents complete financial truth.

  5. For a ready period outside recovery, what happens if a pagination publication revision changes?

    1. Continue with the old cursor
    2. Merge both generations client-side
    3. Return a typed restart and reset pagination

    A cursor must remain bound to one published generation to avoid duplicates and missing rows. During recovery the request stays pending; after reactivation an old cursor requests a restart.

  6. How should migration handle an accepted cutoff whose original observation history was destroyed?

    1. Block and require approved historical evidence or repair
    2. Move the cutoff to the reseed time
    3. Replace the accepted baseline with current totals

    Reconstruction cannot silently manufacture the knowledge that existed at an immutable historical cutoff.

  7. What is the safe rollback model?

    1. Flip back to untouched legacy totals
    2. Fence work, build a fresh target, prove it and atomically switch
    3. Copy old checkpoints onto current totals

    Totals and checkpoints must describe one coherent epoch; mixing them risks double deltas.

  8. What do the local read fixtures not establish?

    1. That the tested returned payload became smaller
    2. That metadata calls increased
    3. Production billing, latency and savings across arbitrary payload sizes

    Deterministic UTF-8 proxies are useful engineering evidence, not deployment measurements or universal size proofs.

  9. What should cleanup do when an old close child belongs to another store?

    1. Delete it to restore progress
    2. Retain it, record the blocked header and let healthy cleanup progress
    3. Stop all reporting lanes

    Tenant safety and queue liveness are both required; foreign data is not disposable repair material.

  10. What remains necessary before claiming production savings?

    1. Approved rollout plus matched whole-pipeline observation and correctness evidence
    2. A green local fixture alone
    3. Opening the worktree report

    This candidate does not authorize production activation or establish matched production results.

  11. What allows safe automatic reactivation after repeated reseeds?

    1. Keeping a pointer to the deleted active epoch
    2. Retained activation history, per-attempt resume policy and a proved new target
    3. Activating as soon as source replay finishes

    History establishes eligibility, the migration establishes intent, and proof establishes correctness. None substitutes for the others.
