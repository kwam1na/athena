A trailing six-month period, and the span routing that made it affordable

Adding a fifth period to Reports is a one-line enum change that would have shipped a preset the days table rejects, a tab with no snapshot behind it, and a mix chart that fails at half-year scale by design. This delivery lands the window last — after raising the drill-down reach, generalizing the range-snapshot lifecycle over its request kind, and moving multi-day SKU mix onto it while keeping single-day mix permanently synchronous.

Executive Summary

Reports offered four periods, the longest a quarter. Owners reviewing seasonality had no half-year view. The change adds Trailing 6 months as a first-class window, raises the drill-down span ceiling so the window is actually explorable, and moves multi-day SKU mix off a synchronous 5,000-row read onto the bounded resumable range-snapshot lifecycle. The three parts ship together because a six-month window that cannot be drilled into, or whose mix chart reliably fails, is worse than no window at all.

The ceiling is 184 days, not 183. These windows are calendar-month aligned, so their length is a sum of six real month lengths. Mar–Aug, May–Oct, Jul–Dec and Aug–Jan each contain 184 inclusive days. A 183-day ceiling would have passed every test written outside those months and rejected real windows in production, date-dependently.

Mix is ambient; movement is on-demand. The mix chart is the Overview's default content — it fires on every visit and every day click. Migrating it wholesale onto admission control and asynchrony would have regressed the workspace's most frequent interaction to fix its rarest. Instead mix is routed by span: spans of at most two days stay on the synchronous reader permanently, and only multi-day ranges reach the snapshot lifecycle.

Nothing was copy-pasted. skuMovementRange.ts held exactly the machinery mix needed, bound to the "sku_movement" literal in every core function. It was generalized into rangeSnapshotLifecycle.ts behind one per-kind config first; mix then landed as configuration, and movement shrank by roughly 570 net lines onto the same rail with no behavior change.

Problem and Context

Three things blocked the one-line version of this change.

The enum is a fan-out. REPORT_OVERVIEW_WINDOWS drove the range-picker presets in ReportDateRangeField and the overview tabs in ReportsOverviewView. Appending the value before the surfaces were ready ships a preset that listDays throws on, a SKU-detail view that rejects the span, and a Units moved sheet whose per-kind ceiling denies admission.

The overview could not see that far. Snapshots are an in-memory filter over one descending read bounded by OVERVIEW_DAY_SCAN_LIMIT = 184 — an exact fit for a three-month window plus its comparison, not a margin. A six-month window alone can consume all 184; with its prior-period comparison it needs about 368.

SKU mix fails at that scale by design. listRangeSkuMix reads reportSkuDay with a 5,000-row cap and throws past it. Production moved 391 distinct SKUs in 30 days (~1,033 rows). A 92-day range is already near the wall; six months clears it routinely.

Mental Model

Three ideas carry the whole delivery.

1. A period is only real when every surface it reaches admits its span

The window enum, the preset list, the tab list, the days table, SKU detail, Units moved and SKU mix are seven surfaces reached by one value. The value lands last. To make that possible, presets were split out of the enum into an explicit REPORT_DATE_RANGE_PRESET_WINDOWS list, so the window could appear on tabs while the picker stayed narrow — and the six-month preset was appended only in the final unit.

2. Ambient work and on-demand work deserve different mechanisms

Units moved opens when an operator asks for it. Mix renders on arrival and on every day click. Admission control, asynchrony and a pending state are correct for the first and a regression for the second. Span routing separates them without a flag, a rollout, or a deprecation.

3. Generalize at the second consumer, not the third

The movement lifecycle's shape was generic; its code guarded on a literal in six core functions. Copying it for mix would have tripled fencing, admission, cleanup and backoff logic and guaranteed they diverge at the first fix applied to one copy.

The 184-Day Arithmetic

This is the detail most likely to be "simplified" by a later reader, so the constant carries its own proof:

Mar–Aug: 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 = 184
May–Oct: 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 30 + 31 = 184
Jul–Dec: 31 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 = 184
Aug–Jan: 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 = 184

trailingSixMonthsStart returns the first day of the month five months back, mirroring trailingThreeMonthsStart. The window is therefore a sum of month lengths, not a fixed day count, and REPORT_TRAILING_SIX_MONTHS_MAX_DAYS = 184 is the true maximum. REPORT_DRILLDOWN_RANGE_MAX_DAYS and REPORT_MIX_RANGE_MAX_DAYS are both derived from it, so a single edit moves every dependent ceiling coherently.

Span Routing, and Why the Sync Path Is Permanent

const isSyncMixSpan =
  inclusiveOperatingDaySpan(skuMixStartDate, skuMixEndDate) <=
  REPORT_SKU_MIX_SYNC_MAX_DAYS; // 2

Two days is not a tuned guess. The fold bounds reportSkuDay at 2,000 rows per operating day, so a two-day span reads at most 4,000 rows — strictly under the synchronous reader's 5,000-row cap. The fast path's safety is derived from an invariant the fold already enforces, which is what makes it a permanent principled path rather than a deprecation waiting to happen.

The consequence at the product level: every day click stays instant and consumes no admission budget, and ambient request volume against the async lane is eliminated at the source rather than tuned around with budgets. The server does not rely on the client's routing — listRangeSkuMix keeps its own 92-day cap, and the async lane enforces 184 through REPORT_RANGE_MAX_DAYS_BY_KIND.sku_mix.

Multi-day mix reuses the sheet's poll-then-subscribe shape: waiting and backpressure write nothing durable, so there is no document to subscribe to. One timer per effect (StrictMode-safe), a server-supplied interval, and dedupe checked before admission budget is charged.

Generalizing the Lifecycle

rangeSnapshotLifecycle.ts owns the kind-generic seams: gate ordering (allowlist → validate → revision vector → dedupe → admission → insert + schedule), phase/fence transitions, retry and backoff, eligible-work scheduling, the sweeper backstop, child-first cleanup, and sanitized error mapping. A RangeSnapshotKindConfig supplies the rest.

Two decisions inside it are load-bearing:

  • hasRankingPhase. A kind may transition aggregating → completed directly. Movement paginates by 20-row rank intervals and needs ranks; mix shows top 5 plus Other, has no ordinal addressing, and therefore has no rank field, no rank pass, and no rank index. Its totals accumulate during aggregation because there is no ranking pass to accumulate in.
  • Historical column names are kept. movementPhase, movementFence, movementTotals and the reportMovementAdmission table are documented as the generic rail for any kind. Renaming stored columns is a migration for a comment's worth of benefit.

The kind registry in the sweeper is deliberately a function, not a module-level const: the sweeper and the kind modules import each other, and a top-level read of a peer's export would race module initialization order.

Exhaustive Exclusion

Three predicates changed from naming a member to testing the dimension:

- if (request.kind === "sku_movement") return;
+ if (request.kind !== undefined) return;

in computeRange, computePendingRanges and expireRangeResults. The old form was correct only while one kind existed. The new form means a kind added next quarter cannot leak into the legacy custom-summary lane by omission — and kindless rows, which are exactly the legacy custom summaries, keep their original semantics untouched.

The Quiet-Store Backfill

getOverview enriches legacy singleton documents at read time. The subtlety is that the sweeper refreshes a store's singleton only when that store has dirty days — so a quiet store's document may never be rewritten, and for that store the read-time backfill is the only source of the new six-month fields. It therefore computes them rather than defaulting to empty snapshots.

The backfill's start date is chosen by "deepest missing field wins": the prior six-month window reaches furthest back, then the prior three-month window, then the three-month window, then the trend. One bounded read of at most OVERVIEW_DAY_SCAN_LIMIT rows fills every shallower gap at once.

Data Model

reportRangeMixSku is a sibling of reportRangeMovementSku, not a reuse of it. Movement's netUnits and absNetUnitsSortKey encode signed movement semantics that mix does not have. Mix children carry unitsSold and a negated unitsSoldSortKey (ascending index order = descending units sold), exist only for SKUs with accumulated unitsSold > 0, and carry their own expiresAt so cleanup finds them without loading a header.

Header totals (totalUnitsSold, skuCount) let the reader derive the Other bucket arithmetically as total minus visible — the Other row has no natural durable representation, and the complete result never crosses to the browser. The reader hydrates at most five SKU identities, after the visible subset is known.

What Intentionally Did Not Change

  • No fold-version bump. Certified fold provenance is already stamped at REPORTS_FOLD_VERSION = 3 across production history, so the new request kind reuses existing provenance. This was verified as a precondition before mix landed, not assumed.
  • The synchronous listRangeSkuMix reader is retained permanently as the ≤2-day path, with its server-side validation unchanged. It is not scheduled for cleanup.
  • ITEMS_PERIOD_MAX_DAYS = 31 is untouched. The Items tab is day/week/month scoped and unaffected.
  • The custom-summary pipeline keeps its 366-day ceiling and 20,000-row behavior, and kindless rows keep their exact original lifecycle including failed-row reuse until TTL.
  • The shared-demo horizon stays at 21 days. Demo's Trailing 3 months already renders from 21 days by convention; Trailing 6 months behaves identically, from derived fixtures, with no live read and no generation authority.
  • No stored-column renames and no admission-table migration. Fixed 10-minute windows age superseded keys out within one window.

Next-Time Guidance

  • Treat a period or scope enum as a fan-out, not a vocabulary. Enumerate every surface that derives from it and land the value last; split "displayed" from "offered" if you need to stage it.
  • Derive calendar-aligned spans arithmetically and write the arithmetic at the constant. A ceiling that is one day short fails only in certain months and reproduces nowhere.
  • Never widen a shared ceiling constant. Parameterize the validator so each call site names its own ceiling, or you will silently widen readers that cannot serve the wider span.
  • Classify the workload as ambient or on-demand before choosing a mechanism. Asynchrony and admission control are correct for on-demand work and a regression for ambient work.
  • Route by span, and prove the fast path's bound from an invariant already enforced elsewhere. A fast path justified by "it usually fits" is a latent outage.
  • Generalize a bounded-work lifecycle at the second consumer, not the third. Copies of fencing, admission, cleanup and backoff diverge at the first fix applied to one copy.
  • Make exclusion predicates exhaustive over the open dimension. Naming known members fails by omission the moment the set grows.
  • A read-time backfill must compute, not default, whenever the background refresh is conditional on tenant activity.
  • Register cross-importing modules through a function, not a const, or module initialization order becomes a correctness dependency.

Comprehension quiz

Pass required: 8 of 10.

  1. Why is the trailing six-month span ceiling 184 days rather than 183?

    1. 184 adds a one-day safety margin so boundary-inclusive arithmetic never rejects a valid window
    2. The window is calendar-month aligned, so its length is a sum of six real month lengths — Mar–Aug, May–Oct, Jul–Dec and Aug–Jan each contain 184 inclusive days
    3. The 184th slot holds the anchor day, which is stored outside the window

    A 183-day ceiling would reject exactly those four runs — a date-dependent failure that looks random in production and reproduces in no test written outside those months. The constant carries the arithmetic so nobody "simplifies" it.

  2. Why was the six-month value appended to REPORT_DATE_RANGE_PRESET_WINDOWS in the final unit rather than with the window enum?

    1. The preset list is generated at build time and could not be updated earlier
    2. Presets are user-facing and needed separate design review
    3. The enum drives the overview tabs while the preset list drives the range picker; decoupling them lets the window exist on tabs before the days table, SKU detail, Units moved and mix all admit 184 days

    The invariant "no preset or tab may exist before every surface it reaches can serve it" then holds at every unit boundary, not merely at ship time.

  3. Why was RANGE_MAX_SPAN_DAYS = 92 renamed and parameterized instead of simply raised to 184?

    1. Four readers shared it with different capabilities; raising it would have widened the synchronous mix and movement readers past the single bounded 5,000-row read they can actually serve
    2. Convex forbids importing a mutable constant across the shared/convex boundary
    3. The constant was needed at two different values within the same query for the current and prior ranges

    requireValidDateRange now takes an explicit maxSpanDays, so listDays and getSkuDetail widen to 184 while listRangeSkuMix and listRangeSkuMovement stay at 92 by construction rather than by luck.

  4. Why does SKU mix route by span instead of moving entirely onto the snapshot lifecycle like Units moved did?

    1. The snapshot lifecycle cannot express a top-N-plus-Other presentation
    2. Shared demo cannot reach async lanes, so a synchronous path had to remain for it
    3. Mix is ambient — it renders on every workspace visit and every day click — so putting all of it behind admission control and a pending state would regress the most frequent interaction to fix the rarest

    Units moved opens only when an operator asks for it. Span routing separates the two workloads without a flag, a rollout, or a deprecation.

  5. What makes the ≤2-day synchronous mix path permanent rather than transitional?

    1. Telemetry shows two-day spans have never approached the row cap in production
    2. The fold bounds reportSkuDay at 2,000 rows per operating day, so a two-day span reads at most 4,000 rows — strictly under the 5,000-row cap, by an invariant already enforced
    3. The synchronous reader falls back to the async lane automatically if it exceeds its cap

    A fast path justified only by "it usually fits" is a latent outage. This one is provable from a bound the fold already guarantees.

  6. Mix has no ranking phase. Why not?

    1. Mix only ever shows top 5 plus Other, so there is no ordinal addressing — children carry a units-sold sort key and the reader takes the top rows through that index at read time
    2. Ranking is deferred to a follow-up unit once the aggregation lane is proven
    3. Convex indexes cannot support a rank field on a second child table

    Movement needs ranks because it paginates by 20-row rank intervals. Because mix has no ranking pass to accumulate in, its header totals are accumulated during aggregation instead, and the last aggregation batch publishes directly.

  7. The sweeper's skips changed from kind === "sku_movement" to kind !== undefined. What does that buy?

    1. It lets legacy custom-summary rows be reclassified as kinded rows over time
    2. It avoids a string comparison in a hot sweep loop
    3. It is exhaustive over the open dimension, so a kind added later cannot leak into the legacy summary compute and expiry lanes by omission

    An exclusion that enumerates known members is correct only until the set grows. Kindless rows — exactly the legacy custom summaries — keep their original semantics untouched.

  8. Why must getOverview's read-time backfill compute the new six-month snapshots rather than default them to empty?

    1. Empty snapshots would fail schema validation on the overview singleton
    2. The sweeper refreshes a store's singleton only when that store has dirty days, so a quiet store's document may never be rewritten — the backfill is that store's only source of the new fields
    3. The tab would render before the singleton refresh completes, causing a visible flicker

    Defaulting would make the new field permanently and silently zero for quiet stores. The backfill picks its start date by "deepest missing field wins", so one bounded read fills every shallower gap.

  9. Why is reportRangeMixSku a new table rather than a reuse of reportRangeMovementSku?

    1. Movement's netUnits and absNetUnitsSortKey mean signed movement, which mix does not have; the sibling reuses the index pattern with honest names and no rank field
    2. Convex tables cannot carry rows from two different request kinds
    3. Sharing the table would have required a migration of existing movement children

    Stored column names on the shared header were deliberately kept historical (movementPhase, movementFence) because renaming them is a migration for a comment's worth of benefit — a different trade-off from inventing false semantics in a new child table.

  10. Why is the sweeper's list of registered kind configs a function rather than a module-level constant?

    1. So kinds can be enabled per store at runtime through the allowlist
    2. So tests can substitute a fake registry without module mocking
    3. The sweeper and the kind modules import each other, so a top-level read of a peer module's export would race module initialization order

    A cyclic import graph makes initialization order a correctness dependency. Deferring the read to call time removes it.
