Schedule-aware end-of-week reporting

How Athena turns bounded Reports projections into an operator briefing without rewriting accepted history

Executive Summary

Athena adds Weekly as a sibling tab between Overview and Items. For verified, allowlisted stores, full administrators can see net sales, units, payment posture, variance, prior-period context, and inventory attention from Reports-owned projections. Weekly is disabled by default; Overview remains the default and keeps its existing behavior.

The reporting frame comes from the effective Store Schedule cycle anchor, closed weekdays, and date exceptions. Operating hours never filter financial facts. Activity on non-operational dates is disclosed outside the reporting schedule instead of being discarded.

A live singleton shows current truth. The final scheduled Daily Close can preserve an immutable accepted baseline, while late facts, reopen, and successor-close changes appear as one current amendment and independent close posture.

Problem and Context

Operators currently have to calculate end-of-week performance manually because source data is too disparate to reason about safely. The briefing must answer five operational questions: money brought in, units moved, payment posture, variances, and inventory uncertainty.

The design had to respect Athena's simplified Reports model and database read discipline. That ruled out client aggregation, source-domain subscriptions, a new reporting generation system, chunk orchestration, and a second scheduler.

Mental Model

Think of the weekly report as three layers of truth. Schedule truth decides which dates are headline dates. Accepted truth records what Athena knew when the final scheduled close was accepted. Current truth records what Athena knows now. These layers are related but never silently overwrite one another.

Store Schedule dates
  -> reportDay folds + bounded Open Work groups
  -> reportDirtyWeek -> existing Reports sweeper
  -> reportWeekCurrent

Final scheduled Daily Close + cutoff facts
  -> immutable reportWeekAccepted

Late facts / reopen / successor close
  -> current replacement + one amendment

Before and After

  • Before: Overview and Items existed, but there was no schedule-aware weekly briefing or accepted weekly record.
  • After: Weekly is additive; /reports still opens Overview, and validated namespaced search state survives a Weekly round trip.
  • Before: operators reconciled sales, payments, variance, and inventory work across domains.
  • After: the server projects those postures together while keeping their meanings distinct and linking back to their owning workspaces.
  • Before: a late fact could only be understood from current source truth.
  • After: the accepted baseline remains intact and current deltas are labeled as an amendment.

Layer-by-Layer Mechanics

  • Schedule: reportingCycleStartsOn is effective-dated. Anchor-changing edits stage at the next boundary under the old anchor; Store Hours shows active and pending anchors and the effective date.
  • Facts and days: observedAt is server-owned, payment allocation posture folds into reportDay, and missing live activity can occupy an explicit zero slot without masking folded evidence gaps.
  • Weekly lifecycle: one current singleton, immutable accepted rows, and one dirty marker are drained by the existing Reports sweeper. The singleton stores available or unavailable posture plus server-owned lifecycle and amendment state.
  • Inventory: live attention uses the canonical bounded synced-sale review grouping; accepted history uses evidence frozen by the final close.
  • Reads: active, paginated history, and exact accepted detail are protected, store-scoped, capability-gated projection queries. Public Weekly reads never hydrate Store Schedule or operational domains.
  • UI: one active-or-detail subscription plus optional lazy history; React formats server-shaped values and does not calculate report meaning.

Failure Boundaries

  • Missing schedule or timezone authority produces unavailable posture.
  • Mixed currency, unknown payment coverage, legacy facts without observedAt, incomplete inventory grouping, and cap overflow stay incomplete rather than becoming exact zero.
  • Currency codes use the shared Reports normalizer at Daily Close emission and again at incremental and authoritative fold comparisons. Case-only drift such as ghs versus GHS remains one currency, while a genuinely foreign code still fails closed.
  • A failed rebuild keeps the last verified current projection and re-marks work for retry. Its stored posture names materializing or cap-specific incomplete state without discarding verified values.
  • Acceptance inserts only after the complete bounded cutoff fold succeeds; repair cannot manufacture accepted history.
  • Authorization and the fail-closed store capability check happen before weekly projection reads.
  • An allowlisted store remains unavailable until the store-wide observedAt verifier records durable zero-missing evidence. Destructive reseed raises a store barrier so partial replay cannot publish current truth, acceptance, or amendments.
  • Daily Close commits independently. A weekly materialization failure cannot roll it back; durable close/day evidence and the existing sweeper retry reporting reconciliation.
  • Store and organization deletion remove weekly rows through bounded scheduled continuations and preserve other stores.

What Intentionally Did Not Change

  • Overview remains the default route with its existing controls, query timing, and hierarchy.
  • Items remains a sibling workspace.
  • Recognized sales, payment allocation, and register variance remain distinct concepts.
  • Reports does not mutate payments, stock, Daily Close, or Open Work.
  • V1 does not add payment-method mix, accounting net profit, cross-store rollups, notifications, generations, chunking, or another scheduler.
  • No screenshot artifact or Cloudflare-hosted plan is part of delivery.

Design decisions taken from the skill

  • Restraint over motion (principle 1, Purpose; §14). The Weekly briefing deliberately contains no springs, gesture-driven transitions, parallax, or translucent material layers. A weekly report is read, not manipulated, so motion would decorate rather than clarify state or hierarchy. This restraint is the design decision, recorded here rather than left as an omission — which is also why the reduced-motion and reduced-transparency rows below are not applicable rather than unverified.
  • Wayfinding and self-links (§16, Familiarity). The workspace tab for the route already showing is now a state-preserving self-link. Rebuilding the active tab's search from the sibling tab's schema silently discarded the selected report, history cursors, and carried Overview return context — a control that looks inert but destroys state is exactly the unpredictability the skill warns against. Covered by ReportsLayout.test.tsx.
  • Feedback must not contradict itself (§16, tactical feedback rules). When completeness reports mixed_currency or fact_cap_exceeded, every figure derived from the withheld totals now renders as an explicit unavailable value instead of printing numbers beneath a sentence saying totals are withheld. Section structure and headings survive so the report stays navigable.
  • Direct, specific labels (§16). Metric labels no longer say "Current" for an accepted, unamended week. The prefix is derived from posture: "Current" only when an amendment supplies later truth, "Accepted" after acceptance, "Week to date" while the week is still live.
  • Typography and grouping over a KPI grid (§15, §16 Simplicity). Net sales stays the single headline at display size with tight leading and negative tracking; supporting lanes use uniform small-caps metric labels and a numeric face, so hierarchy comes from size, weight, and whitespace rather than equally weighted cards. Withheld headline values drop to a smaller muted treatment so an "Unavailable" string does not occupy display-headline space.
  • The headline must be true for the range it is labelled with (§16, Responsibility). Net sales now prints the combined week — scheduled plus outside-schedule — because the label directly above it names seven dates. On the dev store the scheduled-only headline read GH₵101,933 while GH₵31,480 of real sales sat outside it on Sunday: the most prominent number on the screen understated the labelled period, and any reader who did not scroll to Disclosures carried that figure away. The lanes are not merged away — scheduled and outside-schedule stay labelled directly beneath the headline, and variance stays explicitly scheduled-only ("Scheduled close variance") because only a scheduled day has a Daily Close to disagree with.
  • A disclosure belongs beside what it qualifies (§16, Grouping and mapping; Simplicity). The outside-schedule fact moved from the foot of the page to immediately under the headline block. Proximity is what makes a qualifier read as part of the number rather than as a footnote; a fact that changes how a total should be understood cannot sit six sections below it. It states the amount and names the dates in one restrained sentence, appears only when outside-schedule sales exist (a "GH₵0 outside schedule" line would be noise on every ordinary week), and is no longer repeated in Disclosures.
  • Confirmation only where the action is genuinely consequential (§16, Agency). Store Hours now confirms before a save that moves the reporting-cycle anchor, because that save silently defers every other submitted field to a future date. Operational-only saves stay uninterrupted — the skill explicitly warns that overused confirmations train people to click through.
  • Respect the user's text size (§15). Touch-facing report controls are sized with the repo's --control-height-standard (2.75rem / 44px) token rather than a fixed compact height, so the control scales with the type system instead of pinning a pixel box.
  • Status without duplication (§16, Wayfinding). The polite status region announces the weekday-qualified reporting dates plus lifecycle posture, distinct from the plain range printed in the briefing, and is mounted during initial load so the first "Loading" announcement is actually spoken.

Next-Time Guidance

  • Extend the existing fact/day/current/accepted/dirty/sweeper path before proposing new reporting infrastructure.
  • Put period meaning, prior comparisons, completeness, and owner routes in server projections; keep React presentational.
  • Use schedule dates and exceptions for membership, never opening-minute windows for financial eligibility.
  • When historical evidence cannot prove a claim, publish incomplete or unavailable and keep rollout disabled.
  • Run focused sensors during iteration, require constrained reviewer agreement, then spend the full merge gate once.
