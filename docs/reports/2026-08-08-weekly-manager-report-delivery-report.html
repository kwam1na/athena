Executive Summary

Athena now turns the final accepted close of a reporting week into a durable weekly manager report scheduled for 8:00 AM on the following store-local day. The final daily report remains on its existing path, so managers receive the day close first and the weekly synthesis the next morning.

The weekly email is built from the immutable accepted-week baseline, including its top three SKU leaders. Daily reports now use the same top-item presentation posture, report and alert calls to action share an outlined treatment, and operational status copy is calmer and less technical.

Problem and Constraints

Accepted weeks existed as durable reporting truth, but they did not trigger a manager-facing weekly delivery. Reconstructing the email from current SKU-day projections would let late facts change the ranking after acceptance, contradicting the frozen weekly totals.

The sequence also had to preserve the final daily report, schedule in the store timezone, reuse the existing EOD notification category, remain idempotent, and keep missing or legacy evidence honest rather than inventing zeroes.

Mental Model

Acceptance is the evidence boundary; notification delivery is a later operational action. During weekly acceptance, Athena folds cutoff-bounded report facts, freezes totals and the top three positive-unit SKU leaders, persists the accepted baseline, and schedules a notification through the notifications boundary. At send time, the rail loads that baseline, hydrates product display identity, renders the email, and applies the existing subscription, retry, environment, and transport policies.

Only labels are hydrated later. SKU IDs, units, weekly totals, close variance, and other accepted evidence remain fixed. Legacy accepted baselines without frozen leaders omit the top-items section instead of recomputing it from mutable data.

Before and After

  • Before: weekly acceptance persisted a baseline but emitted no manager report. After: it transactionally schedules one for 8 AM the next store-local day.
  • Before: a weekly top-items implementation could drift with reportSkuDay updates. After: leaders are ranked from the same cutoff facts as accepted totals and included in the accepted fingerprint.
  • Before: daily emails omitted item leaders and email CTAs/status blocks varied. After: daily and weekly reports show a consistent top-three pattern and operational templates use the same outlined CTA posture.
  • Before: an open final scheduled day could retain a retry posture that contributed to open/provisional oscillation. After: the open day waits for close acceptance, while historical missing evidence retains bounded recovery.
  • Before: the compact theme switcher exposed a system cycle. After: it presents explicit light and dark choices; system remains available in App Settings.

Implementation Changes

  • Added scheduleNotificationWithCtx so delayed domain notifications still cross the notification-owned boundary.
  • Extended reportWeekAccepted with optional frozen topSkuLeaders and included those leaders in accepted-baseline fingerprinting.

  • Added bounded SKU mix and identity hydration shared by browser and email paths without changing report authority.
  • Simplified the compact theme switcher to light/dark while preserving system selection in the settings surface.

Failure Boundaries

  • Timezone resolution is part of the acceptance mutation; failure aborts the transaction rather than persisting an accepted week with no scheduled delivery.
  • A missing accepted baseline or deleted store makes the notification unsendable; rendering and data-read failures continue through the rail retry contract.
  • Delivery retries are bounded to the rail attempt cap. Intent creation is structurally deduplicated by acceptedWeekId, while an ambiguous provider outcome can still result in a duplicate external email.
  • Product renames or deletions can change or remove hydrated labels, but frozen SKU IDs and unit counts cannot drift.
  • Pre-change accepted baselines are not backfilled and intentionally omit top items because they lack frozen ranking evidence.

Operator and Maintainer Guidance

To verify the sequence, accept the final scheduled close for a store and confirm that the daily report follows its existing delivery path while one weekly emit is scheduled for 8:00 AM the next local day. At delivery time, confirm that the scheduled emit creates or finds the deduplicated notification intent. The email byline includes the acceptance date and time so next-day delivery remains unambiguous.

When evolving the weekly email, add new accepted evidence during materialization and fingerprint it. Do not rebuild frozen claims from current projections at delivery time. Keep new report and alert messages on the notification registry so dedupe, audience, retries, and transport remain centralized.
