The fixture owns history; today belongs to the live rows

The shared demo is a real store. Visitors ring real sales that write real reporting rows — and three surfaces answered from a client fixture whose history ends at yesterday, so the current day rendered as a confident zero. This delivery draws one seam through Reports, the POS Hub and Daily Operations: the fixture answers every date before today, the store’s own rows answer today.

Executive Summary

A visitor could ring GH¢30,360 on the demo POS, walk to Reports, and see GH¢0 · No transactions for today. Same on the POS Hub’s week/month/all-time windows, and on the Daily Operations week rail whenever a past day was selected.

The reason none of this was reported for months is the shape of the wrong value: a zero reads as “nothing happened”, not as “this surface cannot see today”. It is indistinguishable from a genuine quiet day, and the demo store has genuine quiet days — Sundays are closed.

The enabling fact was already true and simply unread: a demo sale runs the same recordFacts ingestion as any other store, patching reportDay and reportSkuDay inside the sale’s own transaction. Nothing needed building; something needed reading.

Problem and Context

Each surface expressed the same architectural mistake through a different mechanism, which is why fixing one did not fix the others.

The POS Hub case is worth dwelling on, because one empty day produced two symptoms. buildStorePulseSummary takes its comparison’s current value from history.at(-1) — which was the empty day. Current 0 against a real prior is exactly −100%, on all four cards at once.

Mental Model

A demo fixture is a history substitute, not the answer. Once a demo store accrues real rows, the fixture’s job is to cover the timeline the database cannot — and that coverage stops at yesterday.

Two supporting facts shaped the implementation:

  • Ingestion has no store gating. The demo’s current day was already materialized in reportDay/reportSkuDay.
  • The sweeper-written artifacts are the wrong source. reportOverview and reportPeriodSkuRollup are rebuilt on a five-minute cron and are allowlist-gated, fail-closed. A visitor who rings a sale must see it now. Reading the incrementally-patched day tables also means the demo store needs no allowlist entry for any of this to work.

What Intentionally Did Not Change

  • History stays fixture-only. No seeded report rows for the 21-day window. The seam is enforced in code: a live payload for any other date is discarded rather than trusted.
  • An open day still has no settled transaction count. reports/transactionCounts.ts states this rule for every store; the demo reports 0 rather than inventing one.
  • The selected historical day’s own metrics. The live day belongs in the week rail, not in the day you are inspecting.
  • No allowlist entry. The sweeper gate is untouched; the feature deliberately does not depend on it.

Operational Guidance

  • The restore registry addition is load-bearing and must ship with the rest. Purging reportDay but leaving reportFact would let the next dirty-day sweep rebuild the deleted revenue from surviving facts.
  • The demo store may hold reporting rows accumulated before this change; the first hourly restore after deploy purges them.
  • reportFact and reportSkuDay carry a raised per-table restore ceiling (2,000) because they are a multiple of the transactions that produce them, and an over-budget throw fails the entire restore.
  • Dirty marks upsert per (store, day), so the demo contributes at most one skipped sweeper mark per hour — and the restore now purges it.

Comprehension quiz

Pass required: 4 of 5.

  1. Why did none of these three zeroes get reported as a bug for months?

    1. They only appeared on mobile viewports.
    2. A zero reads as “nothing happened today”, which is indistinguishable from a genuine quiet day — and the demo store has them.
    3. They were behind a feature flag.
    4. They only occurred after an hourly restore.

    Missing data rendered identically to real data. The general lesson: on any seeded surface, ask whether “nothing happened” and “I cannot see this” render the same.

  2. Why does the live read target reportDay/reportSkuDay rather than reportOverview?

    1. The overview singleton is not indexed by store.
    2. The overview lacks per-SKU rows.
    3. The overview is sweeper-written on a five-minute cron; the day tables are patched inside the sale’s own transaction, so they move the instant a sale completes.
    4. The overview is only written for allowlisted stores.

    A five-minute lag would mean a visitor rings a sale and stares at an unchanged dashboard — worse for the demo than an honest zero. It also means the demo needs no sweeper allowlist entry.

  3. Why must reportFact be purged by the hourly restore, and not just reportDay?

    1. foldDay rebuilds a day from surviving facts, so the next dirty-day sweep would resurrect revenue whose POS rows were already deleted.
    2. Facts are read directly by the Reports UI.
    3. Fact rows would exceed the table size limit.
    4. The fingerprint index would drift.

    Purging the projection but keeping its source means the projection comes back. This is why the registry addition had to ship in the same change as the live read.

  4. Two components derived the same overview document. Convex dedups the read, so what was the harm?

    1. Double the bandwidth.
    2. A stale value in one of them.
    3. A permissions failure on the second read.
    4. Dedup makes the second read free, not simultaneous — the second hook instance settled a commit later, and for that commit its section rendered null while siblings painted at the top of the page.

    Measured 0.21 CLS. The fix passes the settled document down as a prop so there is one source and one settle.

  5. Why was FlipNumber flipping numbers on first page load?

    1. Its layout effect ran twice under StrictMode.
    2. The house pattern value={total ?? 0} mounts a placeholder zero, so the real figure is mechanically the first change — and transitionFromZero defaulted to "flip".
    3. The formatter identity changed each render.
    4. Reduced-motion was misdetected.

    58 of 67 call sites took the default; nine had already opted into "fade" individually. The default is now "fade", and a change between two real values still flips.
