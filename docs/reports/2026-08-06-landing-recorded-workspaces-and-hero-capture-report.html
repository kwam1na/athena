Summary

The landing page told its story with static screenshots. Three of those exhibits are now recorded clips of the real workspaces, and a new Reports act carries the narrative past a single day. Alongside that, the hero got a grid field, a pointer tilt, and a rebuilt entrance animation.

The most consequential find was not on the landing page at all. Chasing a report that the hero screenshot looked clipped on the right, pixel sampling showed card borders painted on their left edge and simply absent on their right. The cause is the authenticated app's zoom: 0.95: under fractional zoom Chrome rasterizes a 1px border to less than one device pixel and, depending on where the edge falls, rounds it away entirely. The capture script now pins zoom to 1. That fixes the screenshots; it does not fix the app, which is why a follow-up is called out in the guidance section.

Problem

Three separate problems arrived as one request to "update the story with the videos".

The story was static. Every exhibit was a PNG pair. The copy claimed numbers "keep themselves current" and that sales sync on their own, but nothing on the page moved to show it, and no act covered Reports at all.

The hero entrance stuttered. The eyebrow, headline, subhead, and CTA each animated separately, staggered 280ms apart over 1.4–1.5s, and each tween animated filter: blur(). Up to four concurrent blurs ran over large overlapping text, and the stagger read as four arrivals rather than one motion.

The hero screenshot was stale and then wrong. The Daily Operations workspace had consolidated its automation and open-register sections into a single "Store day status" card, so the committed shot no longer matched the app. Re-capturing surfaced two further faults: the capture session rendered the nav sidebar expanded where the original had it collapsed, and card borders went missing on one edge.

Mental model

Think of a screenshot capture as a third rendering target, alongside the browser and the test runner. It has its own viewport, its own device pixel ratio, its own persisted UI state, and its own zoom. Any of those can differ from what a developer sees locally, and each difference is invisible until it shows up baked into a PNG.

Three of this branch's bugs are the same shape: state the capture inherited rather than declared.

  • Sidebar state lives in a sidebar_state cookie. The capture inherited whatever the demo session last had.
  • App zoom is 0.95 on desktop. The capture inherited it, and fractional zoom is hostile to hairlines.
  • Crop height was a constant tuned against a layout that had since changed, so it cut through the wrong section.

The general rule the script now follows: declare every input that affects the pixels. Theme was already declared. Sidebar and zoom now are. Crop height cannot be declared away, so it carries a comment saying how to re-measure it rather than a bare magic number.

What changed, and what deliberately did not

Changed

  • Videos re-encoded from the supplied solid-background recordings to H.264 with audio stripped and faststart: daily-ops 5.7MB → 2.0MB, reports 3.3MB → 1.2MB, POS 720KB → 230KB per variant, each with a poster frame.
  • The nav underline is transparent at the top of the page and drawn once scrolled, fading on --motion-slow while the background wash keeps --motion-standard.
  • The hero entrance animates the copy as one layer, and is gated behind HERO_ENTRANCE_ENABLED, currently false.
  • The capture script pins zoom and sidebar state, and its clip-height default carries a comment on how to re-measure rather than a bare constant.

Deliberately not changed

  • The app's zoom: 0.95. Pinning zoom in the capture fixes screenshots only. Whether real desktop users lose hairline borders is a separate question with its own blast radius, tracked as follow-up rather than fixed opportunistically inside a landing-page branch.
  • The nav demo CTA on other public pages. It is gated per page, not deleted. The privacy and walkthrough pages assert it is visible, and it is their only demo entry point.
  • The EOD Review and Opening Handoff shots. Their crop ends exactly on main's content-box right edge, so flush elements have their borders sliced. A fix was written, reviewed against the actual rendering, and then reverted when the reported symptom turned out to be elsewhere. Noted, not smuggled in.
  • The hero shot's scroll-linked scale. Only the opacity ramp was removed; the shot still grows from 0.9 to 1 on scroll.

Validation

  • Border fix proven by pixel sampling, not by eye. A row through the "Store day status" card was decoded to raw RGB. Before: left edge (230,233,236), right edge no border pixel at all. After: the border colour appears at both x=432 and x=3502, and the tiles row shows (220,223,229) on both outer edges.
  • Entrance measured, not asserted. A frame monitor installed on window survived a client-side route change, so the entrance could be replayed and sampled: 174 frames, 8.33ms mean, 9.2ms p95, 0 frames over 20ms, 0 long tasks, with blur sampled stepping 8 → 6.7 → 4.9 → 3.4 → 2.2 → 1.25 → 0.
  • A false green was caught by that same instrumentation. The first measurement reported a flawless 120fps while the copy sat frozen at blur(6px), opacity: 0 — a stale HMR module had thrown the route into its error boundary. Frame timing alone would have been read as success.
  • Video behaviour verified live: all three clips resumed at their carried playhead across a theme flip (14.25s, 9.1s, 5.4s each preserved), and sources and posters swapped together.
  • tsc --noEmit clean and the landing, privacy, and walkthrough suites green throughout. Two tsc runs failed mid-session with a JavaScript heap OOM under memory pressure; they pass with a raised heap, and that is an environment limit rather than a type error.

Guidance for next time

  • Sample pixels before theorising about a visual bug. Several plausible explanations were entertained for the missing borders — crop geometry, device-pixel alignment, an overflow ancestor — and a viewport sweep disproved the alignment theory outright. One row of RGB settled it in a single command.
  • Instrument the property you are tuning, not just the frame rate. Frame timing said "smooth" for an animation that was not running at all. Sampling the computed filter alongside the frame deltas is what made the measurement trustworthy.
  • A capture script should declare its inputs. Theme was declared; sidebar and zoom were not, and both silently changed the output. When adding a new capture, ask what persisted state the page reads at load.
  • Follow-up owed: determine whether zoom: 0.95 drops hairline borders for real desktop users at common viewport and DPR combinations. The capture-side pin does not help them.

Comprehension quiz

Five questions on the reasoning behind this branch.

  1. Why were the card borders missing on one edge of the hero screenshot?

    1. The crop ended before the card's right edge.
    2. An ancestor with overflow hidden clipped the border.
    3. The app renders at zoom: 0.95, so a 1px border becomes less than one device pixel and rounds away depending on where the edge falls.
    4. The card's CSS omitted a right border.

    The affected elements reported a computed border width of 1.05263px — exactly 1 ÷ 0.95. The crop had over 200 CSS px of margin beyond the card, and the nearest clipping ancestor ended at the viewport edge, so neither of those explanations survived measurement.

  2. What made the hero entrance stutter, given that the blur was kept in the final version?

    1. The blur radius was too large at 8px.
    2. Four separately staggered layers ran up to four concurrent blur repaints over overlapping text.
    3. anime.js cannot animate the filter property.
    4. The animation ran before fonts had loaded.

    Collapsing the copy into a single animated container makes the frost one rasterization instead of four. With that change the original 8px radius measured zero dropped frames, which is why the blur could stay.

  3. A frame monitor reported 8.33ms mean with zero dropped frames on the first entrance measurement. Why was that result rejected?

    1. The sample size of 174 frames was too small.
    2. The display refreshes at 120Hz, so 8.33ms is the floor.
    3. Sampled alongside the frames, the copy sat at blur(6px) and opacity: 0 the whole time — a stale module had broken the route, so nothing was animating.
    4. The monitor did not survive the route change.

    A perfect frame rate is exactly what you get when no work happens. Measuring the animated property alongside the timing is what distinguished a smooth animation from an absent one.

  4. Why was the nav's "Try the demo" button gated per page rather than deleted outright?

    1. Deleting it would have broken the funnel event schema.
    2. It is the only demo entry point on the privacy and walkthrough pages, which assert it is visible; only the landing carries its own demo CTAs.
    3. The layout reserves space for it on every page.
    4. It is required for the landing funnel to record a page view.

    PublicLayout is shared. A global deletion would have stripped two other public pages of their only route into the demo and broken the tests encoding that intent.

  5. What is the general lesson the sidebar, zoom, and clip-height bugs share?

    1. Headless Chromium renders differently from a real browser.
    2. Screenshots should be captured at a device pixel ratio of 1.
    3. A capture inherits persisted state and layout constants it does not declare, and every undeclared input can silently change the pixels.
    4. Product screenshots should be regenerated on every commit.

    Theme was declared and behaved. Sidebar state came from a cookie, zoom from a class on the document root, and the crop height from a constant tuned against an older layout — all three inherited, all three wrong.
